COUNTDOWN
A paper screen for the hallway

Know when to leave before you've found your keys.

Countdown is a 7.5-inch e-paper display that shows your next departures, the weather, anything disrupting your day, what's playing on Spotify and your energy use. It reads like a printed page, has no backlight, and you set it up from your phone.

Departures

London buses and the Tube. Oslo's metro and trams. On one screen.

Pick your stops and they're shown in the order you choose, live and counting down in minutes, with the next departure first and the ones after it lined up underneath.

  • London stops come from TfL: buses with their stop letter, Tube stations by line.
  • Norwegian stops come from Entur, so Ruter and every other Norwegian operator is covered. Add a whole station or a single platform.
  • Mix London and Norway in the same list if your life spans both.
  • More stops than fit? The screen pages through them, and small squares show which ones are on now.

Elephant & Castle and Kennington, in London.

Weather

Now, tonight, tomorrow and the weekend.

The weather panel turns through four pages: what it's like now, tonight's low, tomorrow's high, and both days of the coming weekend. Sunrise, sunset, humidity and wind are there when the screen has room.

Forecasts come from Open-Meteo for the town or postcode you type. On first setup the site suggests your nearest town, worked out on the server without sending your IP address to anyone.

The weather panel as a portrait screen draws it.

Notice board

The things that change your plans, ranked by how much they matter.

Give it your postcode and the notice board gathers what's relevant near you. The most serious notices go first; planned works only fill the rows left over.

  • UK: TfL line status and station problems for your stops, Met Office weather warnings, Environment Agency flood alerts, TfL road incidents nearby, bank holidays and the clocks changing.
  • Norway: MET Norway weather warnings in your screen's language, public holidays, the clocks changing, and disruptions on your Entur stops.

An orange wind warning and a tram disruption, in the card style.

Spotify and energy

What's playing, what's next, and what the house is using.

Spotify. Connect your account once and the screen shows the track that's playing, with its album art and what's up next. It takes turns with your top five tracks and artists of the last four weeks and the last twelve months. Your Spotify login stays on the server; the screen never holds it.

Energy. With a Glowmarkt smart-meter account, the screen charts your electricity use over the last 24 hours, and pages on to this month and this year.

Make it yours

Three looks, two languages, either way up.

One style dresses the whole screen. New screens start on Airy, with light labels like the weather panel. Hero has bold headers and makes the first item big, and Card puts each panel in a rounded card. The settings page shows a preview of each before you save. Choose a 24-hour or 12-hour clock, English or Norwegian text, and hang the screen landscape or portrait.

Setup

Plug it in. Everything else happens on your phone.

  1. Join it to your Wi-FiThe screen opens its own Wi-Fi setup page on first boot.
  2. Sign in on the page it opensYour browser lands on the pairing page and the screen becomes yours. A short code on the screen works as a fallback.
  3. Give it a nameOne screen or several, each with its own settings.
  4. Choose your stops and weatherSearch stations in London or Norway, set your postcode, and save. Changes reach the screen on its next refresh, and the settings page can show you what the screen is showing right now.

Calm by design

A screen that stays out of the way.

E-paper holds its picture without power to the pixels, so there's no glow in a dark hallway. A new picture is only made when something on it actually changes.
